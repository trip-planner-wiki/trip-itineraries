# Kifune & Kurama: Mountain Shrines & Onsen

**City:** Kyoto  
**Best for:** Shrine visits, nature, onsen, spiritual atmosphere  
**Approximate duration:** Full day (7–8 hours)  
**Estimated cost:** ¥4,000–¥8,000

---

## Overview

[![Kifune Shrine stone lantern approach, Kyoto](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f5/Sand%C5%8D%2C_Kifune_Shrine%2C_Kyoto_-_Jul_22%2C_2007.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sand%C5%8D,_Kifune_Shrine,_Kyoto_-_Jul_22,_2007.jpg)

Northern Kyoto's Kifune and Kurama valleys are among the most atmospheric destinations in the entire country — a world away from the city bustle, accessible in under an hour by train. Kurama has an ancient temple complex and the legendary Kurama Onsen. Kifune Shrine is an ancient water shrine with a mystical mountain atmosphere. Together they make for a spiritual, nature-immersed, and deeply restorative day. Kifune Shrine, formally known as Kibune-jinja, has been venerated for over 1,600 years as a deity of water and rain; its rows of stone lanterns flanking the moss-covered approach are particularly striking when lit at dusk. Kurama-dera, founded in 770 CE, sits atop a mountain whose old-growth cedar forest creates one of the most immersive sacred environments in the entire Kyoto region.

## Itinerary

### Morning — Eizan Railway to Kurama (09:00)
From Demachiyanagi Station (end of Keihan Line), take the charming Eizan Electric Railway (Kurama Line) to Kurama Station (~30 minutes, ¥480). The train runs through forests and is a lovely journey itself.

### Morning — Kurama-dera Temple (09:45)
**📍 1074 Kuramahonmachi, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto**

Kurama-dera is an ancient mountain temple complex reached by a 30-minute hike (or cable car for ¥200) up through old-growth cedar forest. The temple sits atop Mount Kurama and is famous for its powerful spiritual atmosphere — it is the legendary birthplace of Reiki energy healing. Explore the main hall, the outdoor stone platforms, and the forest trail. Allow 1.5–2 hours.

### Lunch — Kurama Riverside Restaurants (12:00)
The village of Kurama at the base of the mountain has several traditional restaurants serving river fish, tofu cuisine, and set meals. Budget ¥1,500–¥2,500.

### Mid-Afternoon — Kurama Onsen (13:30)
**📍 520 Kuramahonmachi, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto**

Kurama Onsen is one of the most well-regarded onsen in the Kyoto area — sodium bicarbonate waters said to be excellent for the skin. The outdoor bath (rotenburo) is surrounded by trees and has a beautiful mountain atmosphere. Entry for outdoor bath only: ¥1,500. Bring a towel or rent one. Allow 1–1.5 hours to relax properly.

### Late Afternoon — Hike to Kifune (15:30)
From Kurama Onsen, experienced hikers can take the 2km forest trail over the mountain to Kifune (~1 hour walk). The trail is well-maintained but has some elevation — wear comfortable shoes. Alternatively, return to Kurama Station and take the Eizan Railway one stop to Kibuneguchi (¥150), then walk 30 min up the river valley to Kifune Shrine.

### Evening — Kifune Shrine (16:30)
**📍 180 Kibune-cho, Kurama, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto**

Kifune Shrine is an ancient mountain water shrine dedicated to the god of water. The stone lantern-lined approach through the forest is one of the most atmospheric sights in Kyoto. Famous for kawadoko — seasonal riverside platform dining (June–September). Explore the three shrine compounds: Kifune Shrine (main), Yui Shrine (middle), and Okumiya (innermost). Free entry to grounds.

### Return to Kyoto (17:30)
Walk down to Kibuneguchi Station and take the Eizan Railway back to Demachiyanagi, then connect to central Kyoto.

## Tips
- The Kifune Matsuri (June 1) is a spectacular festival at Kifune Shrine — see the dedicated event page
- Kurama Onsen has locker rooms and towel rental — you don't need to bring much
- The hike between Kurama and Kifune is beautiful but can be slippery in rain — check the forecast
- Weekdays are significantly less crowded at both Kurama and Kifune
