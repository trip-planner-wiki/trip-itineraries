# Tokyo Hidden Gems: A Day Trip Through Temples, Shrines & Nostalgic Alleys

**City:** Tokyo
**Best for:** temples, shrines, history, street food, evening drinks
**Approximate duration:** Full day (10–12 hours)

## Overview

This day trip takes you off the well-worn tourist trail and into three of Tokyo's most characterful corners. Begin your morning at Gotokuji Temple in the quiet residential neighbourhood of Setagaya — a Zen Buddhist temple blanketed with hundreds of white ceramic lucky cats. From there, cross the city to Hie Shrine in Akasaka, where a tunnel of vermilion torii gates climbs through a hillside garden that feels worlds away from the surrounding government district. End the evening as Tokyo comes alive at Omoide Yokocho in Shinjuku — a narrow, lantern-lit alleyway packed with yakitori smoke, cold beer, and decades of nostalgic atmosphere. The pace is relaxed throughout, with plenty of time to linger at each stop.

### Morning — Gotokuji Temple (09:00)

[![Gotokuji Temple — rows of white maneki-neko lucky cat figurines](https://dashboard.japantravel.com/photo/60829-207532/1200x630!/tokyo-tokyo-s-gotokuji-temple-207532.webp)](https://en.japantravel.com/tokyo/tokyo-s-gotokuji-temple/60829 "Gotokuji Temple — rows of white maneki-neko lucky cat figurines")

**📍 2-24-7 Gotokuji, Setagaya-ku, Tokyo 154-0021**

Gotokuji is Tokyo's legendary "lucky cat temple" — a serene Soto Zen Buddhist complex said to be the birthplace of the iconic maneki-neko (beckoning cat) good luck figurine. Hundreds of white ceramic cats, each donated by a visitor, are arranged in tiered clusters on a raised platform inside the grounds, creating one of the most photogenic and quietly magical scenes in the city. Beyond the cats, the temple itself is beautiful: a three-story pagoda, a Zen cemetery shaded by ancient trees, and a calm that feels a world away from central Tokyo. Arrive early (from 09:00) to enjoy the grounds before tour groups arrive. Allow **45–60 minutes**.

- **Entry:** Free
- **Opening hours:** 6:00–18:00

### Late Morning — Hie Shrine (11:00)

[![Hie Shrine — tunnel of red torii gates on the hillside approach](https://dashboard.japantravel.com/photo/44971-185065/1200x630!/tokyo-hie-shrine-on-a-rainy-day-185065.webp)](https://en.japantravel.com/tokyo/hie-shrine-on-a-rainy-day/44971 "Hie Shrine — tunnel of red torii gates on the hillside approach")

**📍 2-10-5 Nagata-cho, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0014**

Tucked between the Tokyo government district and the Akasaka embassy quarter, Hie Shrine is one of the capital's most important Shinto shrines — originally established to protect Edo Castle. Its most striking feature is the serpentine tunnel of red torii gates that lines the eastern hillside staircase leading up from the street below, reminiscent of Kyoto's Fushimi Inari but far less crowded. At the top, the main shrine hall is framed by ancient zelkova trees, and the grounds include a small sub-shrine dedicated to the god of harvest. The atmosphere here is calm and ceremonial. After exploring the grounds, stroll down the broad stone staircase at the main entrance and take in the contrast with the modern city skyline behind you. Allow **45–60 minutes**.

- **Entry:** Free
- **Opening hours:** Apr–Sep 5:00–18:00 / Oct–Mar 6:00–17:00

After your visit, you have a relaxed afternoon to explore the surrounding Akasaka neighbourhood. The area has excellent lunch options — try one of the small ramen shops or tonkatsu restaurants tucked along the backstreets near Akasaka-Mitsuke station (budget ¥1,000–¥1,500).

### Evening — Omoide Yokocho (18:00)

[![Omoide Yokocho — lantern-lit alleyway with yakitori smoke in Shinjuku](https://dashboard.japantravel.com/photo/5025-32798/1200x630!/tokyo-shinjuku-s-memory-lane-omoide-yokocho-32798.webp)](https://en.japantravel.com/tokyo/shinjuku-s-memory-lane-omoide-yokocho/5025 "Omoide Yokocho — lantern-lit alleyway with yakitori smoke in Shinjuku")

**📍 1-chome Nishi-Shinjuku, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo (west exit of Shinjuku Station)**

Wait until 18:00 before heading in — this is when Omoide Yokocho truly comes alive. Two narrow alleyways wedged beside the JR Shinjuku train tracks house around 80 tiny bars and grilled-meat stalls that have barely changed since the postwar era. Squeeze into a counter seat at one of the standing yakitori stalls, order skewers of charcoal-grilled chicken, and wash it down with a cold draft beer or a warming glass of sake. The atmosphere is thick with smoke, laughter, and the satisfying hiss of fresh skewers hitting the grill. This is old Tokyo at its most vivid. Allow **75–90 minutes**, or longer if you feel like hopping to a second stall.

- **Entry:** Free to enter
- **Opening hours:** ~15:00–midnight (varies by stall)
- **Budget:** ¥1,500–¥3,000 per person for drinks and food — **cash only**

## Tips

- **Arrive at Gotokuji early** — by 09:00–09:30 the grounds are peaceful and ideal for photos. Weekday mornings are especially quiet.
- **Omoide Yokocho is strictly cash only** — bring at least ¥3,000 per person in cash before heading in. There are ATMs inside Shinjuku Station's west exit area.
- **Use the afternoon gap wisely** — the two to three hours between Hie Shrine and Omoide Yokocho's prime evening hours are perfect for exploring Akasaka, having a relaxed sit-down lunch, or a short visit to the nearby National Diet Building garden (free, open weekdays).
- **Wear comfortable shoes** — Gotokuji's temple grounds and Hie Shrine's torii-gate staircase both involve uneven stone paths.
- **IC card (Suica or Pasmo)** is the easiest way to pay for all transit on this route. Load at least ¥1,500 before departing.
