# Tokyo Day Trip: Asakusa & Akihabara

**City:** Tokyo
**Best for:** temples, pop culture, retro gaming, maid cafes
**Approximate duration:** Full day (7–9 hours)
**Estimated cost:** ¥3,000–¥6,000 (transit + arcade games + maid cafe entry and food/drink)

---

## Overview

This is one of those days that captures two completely different sides of Tokyo in a single, unhurried outing. You'll start your morning in **Asakusa**, one of the city's oldest and most atmospheric neighbourhoods, where the ancient Senso-ji Temple rises up amidst the smoke of incense sticks and the soft chatter of early-morning pilgrims. Then you'll make your way to **Akihabara** — the undisputed capital of Japanese pop culture — to blast through rounds at TAITO STATION's legendary arcade before settling in for an afternoon at one of Tokyo's most beloved maid cafes. Expect a day that moves from serene to electric, with plenty of charm at every stop.

### Morning — Senso-ji Temple (06:00)

[![Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Sensoji_temple%2C_Asakusa%2C_Tokyo%2C_Japan.jpg/1280px-Sensoji_temple%2C_Asakusa%2C_Tokyo%2C_Japan.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sensoji_temple,_Asakusa,_Tokyo,_Japan.jpg "Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan")

**📍 2-3-1 Asakusa, Taito-ku, Tokyo 111-0032**

Tokyo's oldest temple is at its most magical in the early morning, before the tour groups arrive and while the incense smoke still drifts lazily through cool air. Enter through the iconic **Kaminarimon Gate** (Thunder Gate) — the great red paper lantern hanging at its centre is one of the most photographed sights in all of Japan — and walk up the bustling **Nakamise-dori** shopping street toward the main hall. Pick up some traditional snacks (ningyo-yaki, a sweet red bean cake shaped like a pigeon, is a local favourite) and browse the souvenir stalls for everything from folding fans to daruma dolls.

At the main hall, toss a coin in the offertory box, bow twice, clap twice, and bow once more. If you're feeling lucky, draw an *omikuji* (fortune slip) from one of the wooden cylinders — and if it says bad luck, tie it to a rack near the hall to leave your bad fortune behind. Allow yourself time to also explore the quieter parts of the complex: the five-storied pagoda and the smaller Asakusa Jinja shrine next door are often overlooked and well worth a few minutes.

**Admission:** Free | **Suggested time:** 1.5–2 hours

### Late Morning / Early Afternoon — TAITO STATION Akihabara (10:00)

[![TAITO STATION Akihabara arcade interior](https://www.taito.co.jp/Content/images/zone/0/201407/2b46f5db-21f0-4ff6-9cd5-75f329161afe_m_01_ja.jpg)](https://www.taito.co.jp/en/store/00001802 "TAITO STATION Akihabara")

**📍 Kisan Building, 4-2-2 Sotokanda, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 101-0021**

Step off the train and into a completely different universe. TAITO STATION Akihabara is a multi-floor arcade packed with crane games, rhythm machines, and classic cabinet games — it's one of the most prominent game centres in a neighbourhood already overflowing with them. The thrill of a well-aimed crane game grab is universal, but if you're a fan of retro gaming, keep an eye out for original Space Invaders cabinets (Space Invaders is a TAITO creation, after all — they invented the coin-op shooter). Medals and IC-style tokens make the rhythm and medal games even more accessible. Come with a budget in mind and pace yourself — it's easy to lose track of time here.

**Admission:** Free entry (pay per game) | **Hours:** 10:00–24:00 | **Suggested time:** 1–2 hours | **Estimated spend:** ¥500–¥2,000

### Afternoon / Evening — @home cafe – Akihabara Main Store (13:00–15:00)

[![@home cafe Akihabara maid cafe](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/6690f4314988280c039604c8/490c4acf-7ef6-4f6e-a83f-0516336a9bc0/IMG_2787_result.jpg?format=1500w&content-type=image%2Fjpeg)](https://maidcafe-athome.com/ "@home cafe – Akihabara's most iconic maid cafe")

**📍 Mitsuwa Building 3F–7F, 1-11-4 Sotokanda, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 101-0021**

@home cafe is one of Akihabara's most established and beloved maid cafes — it has been running since 2004 and consistently earns its reputation as the friendliest introduction to the genre for first-timers. Staff greet guests as "Master" or "Princess" and go out of their way to create a warm, theatrical atmosphere across multiple themed floors. The menu includes kawaii-decorated omurice (you can have a maid draw a message on your ketchup), parfaits, pasta, and drinks — all presented with enthusiasm and ceremony.

The entry system is simple: pay ¥780 per hour (per person) and order at least one food or drink item. Staff speak English, Japanese, Chinese, and Korean, so language is no barrier. Don't leave without asking for your complimentary **"Master's Certificate"** souvenir — it's a cheerful memento that makes a surprisingly fun keepsake.

**Admission:** ¥780/hour + 1 food/drink minimum | **Hours:** 10:00–22:00 (last order 21:20) | **Suggested time:** ~1 hour | **Estimated spend:** ¥2,000–¥3,500 (entry + food/drink)

## Tips

- **Arrive at Senso-ji before 08:00** — the temple grounds are open 24 hours and the main hall opens at 06:00. Arriving early means beating the tour groups and experiencing a rare tranquillity at one of Tokyo's most-visited sites.
- **Use a Suica or Pasmo IC card** for seamless transit between Asakusa and Akihabara. Tap in and out at every gate — no need to buy individual tickets.
- **Bring cash** — TAITO STATION arcade machines and some stalls on Nakamise-dori are cash only. The ¥100 coin is your best friend in an arcade.
- **Book @home cafe in advance or arrive early** — weekends and holidays can mean a wait. Walk-ins are accepted but tables can fill up. Check [cafe-athome.com](https://www.cafe-athome.com/en/) for online reservations.
- **Senso-ji is a living place of worship** — be respectful around the main hall: speak quietly, avoid blocking worshippers, and don't flash photography inside.
