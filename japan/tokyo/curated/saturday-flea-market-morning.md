# A Saturday Flea Market Morning in Tokyo

**City:** Tokyo  
**Best for:** Vintage shopping, antiques, local culture, flea markets, budget travel  
**Approximate duration:** Half day (09:00–14:00, ~5 hours)  
**Estimated cost:** ¥1,500–¥5,000/person (free entry + food + any purchases)

---

## Overview

Every weekend — almost without exception — the parking lot of Oi Racecourse in Shinagawa transforms into Tokyo's largest flea market. The Tokyo City Flea Market (大井競馬場フリーマーケット) fields 300–500 vendor stalls spread across a vast outdoor lot, making it the biggest of its kind in the city by a wide margin. This is not a tourist attraction: you'll find local families selling off household collections, seasoned dealers hawking antiques, and everything from kimono and vintage denim to porcelain tanuki and 1980s electronics. Admission is free, bargaining is welcome, and the entire experience is over by lunchtime — leaving the afternoon wide open. This is one of the most genuine, unhurried ways to spend a Tokyo morning.

### Morning — Tokyo City Flea Market at Oi Racecourse (09:00)

[![Tokyo City Flea Market at Oi Racecourse — vendor stalls spread across the vast parking lot](https://i0.wp.com/itravelblog.net/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/16450-20170801-2.jpg)](https://en.itravelblog.net/tokyo-city-flea-market/ "Tokyo City Flea Market — Oi Racecourse Flea Market guide on iTravelBlog")

**📍 2-1-2 Katsushima, Shinagawa, Tokyo (Parking lot of Oi Racecourse / Tokyo City Keiba)**  
**Admission:** Free  
**Hours:** Saturday & Sunday, 09:00–14:30 (cancelled on rainy days — check Instagram @tokyocity_fleamarket before heading out)  
**Instagram:** https://www.instagram.com/tokyocity_fleamarket/

Step off the Tokyo Monorail at **Ōi Keibajō-mae Station** and follow the steady stream of locals heading to the racecourse parking lot — you'll reach the market entrance in about 5 minutes on foot. With 300–500 stalls filling the asphalt expanse every weekend, this is Tokyo's biggest flea market by a significant margin. Arrive as close to 09:00 as you can: experienced treasure-hunters and dealers start early, and the best finds often go quickly.

**What to expect:**

- **Clothing** — The most popular category. Expect kimono, yukata, vintage denim, '70s and '80s Japanese fashion, wool coats, and leather goods. Prices typically run ¥200–¥3,000 for garments.
- **Antiques and collectibles** — Porcelain, lacquerware, Showa-era toys, vintage cameras, old film posters, and curiosities you can't name but can't put down.
- **Household goods** — Ceramics, glassware, kitchen tools, furniture, books, and records.
- **Handmade goods and accessories** — Jewellery, bags, and small crafts from independent makers.
- **Food trucks** — A cluster of food stalls near the entrance serves coffee, taiyaki, and light snacks. Grab a coffee and settle in for a slow browse.

**Bargaining tips:** Vendors here expect and welcome gentle negotiation. A polite smile and "motto yasuku narimasuka?" ("could it be a little cheaper?") works well. Don't push hard on items clearly marked with firm prices; accept gracefully when a vendor says no. Most deals are cash only — bring plenty of small bills (¥500, ¥1,000).

Plan to spend **2.5–3 hours** working through the stalls at a comfortable pace. The market winds down noticeably after 13:00 as vendors begin packing up.

### Late Morning / Lunch — Oimachi Station Area (12:30)

[![Oimachi Station shopping area, Shinagawa — local restaurants and covered arcades near Tokyo Bay](https://itravelblog.net/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/16450-20170801-5.jpg)](https://en.itravelblog.net/tokyo-city-flea-market/ "The Oimachi area near Shinagawa")

**📍 Oimachi Station, Shinagawa-ku, Tokyo**

After the market, head to **Oimachi Station** for lunch. The Oimachi shopping streets (covered arcades running south from the station) are packed with unpretentious local spots: ramen shops, teishoku (set meal) restaurants, soba houses, and bakeries. This is a working-class residential neighbourhood — prices are low and queues are short compared to tourist areas. Budget ¥800–¥1,500 for a very satisfying lunch.

A few things to look for:
- **Ramen** — Several well-regarded local shops are clustered in the covered arcade; look for the ones with handwritten menus and counter seating.
- **Teishoku** — Grilled fish or tonkatsu set meals with rice, miso soup, and pickles for under ¥1,000.
- **Standing sushi bars** — Quick, fresh, and cheap; perfect for a light post-market meal.

After lunch, the afternoon is yours: you're just one stop from **Shinagawa Station** with direct Shinkansen access if you're heading onward, or a short ride back into central Tokyo.

## Tips

- **Always check Instagram before you go** — The market is cancelled on rainy days without exception. Check [@tokyocity_fleamarket](https://www.instagram.com/tokyocity_fleamarket/) the evening before and again on the morning of your visit.
- **Bring cash in small denominations** — Virtually all transactions are cash only. Bring ¥5,000–¥15,000 in a mix of ¥500 and ¥1,000 notes depending on how much you plan to buy. ATMs at Ōi Keibajō-mae Station are limited; top up at a convenience store ATM before you go.
- **Arrive early for the best selection** — The 09:00–11:00 window is prime time. Later arrivals still find plenty, but popular clothing vendors and antique stalls thin out quickly.
- **Bring a reusable bag or small backpack** — Vendors don't reliably provide bags and you'll want both hands free for digging through stalls.
- **Getting there:** Take the **Tokyo Monorail Haneda Airport Line** to **Ōi Keibajō-mae Station** (Ōi Racecourse Station). From Hamamatsuchō it's about 12 minutes; from Haneda Airport it's a convenient stop en route to central Tokyo. The racecourse parking lot entrance is a 5-minute walk from the station.
