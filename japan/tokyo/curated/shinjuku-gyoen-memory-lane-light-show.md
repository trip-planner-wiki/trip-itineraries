# A Perfect Day in Shinjuku: Gardens, Yakitori & Projection Mapping

**City:** Tokyo (Shinjuku)
**Best for:** nature, food, architecture, evening light shows
**Approximate duration:** Full day (9–10 hours)
**Estimated cost:** ¥2,000–¥5,000 (entry ¥500, dinner ¥1,500–¥3,500, observatory free)

---

## Overview

This user-curated day starts with a leisurely morning stroll through one of Tokyo's most beautiful imperial gardens, transitions into a memorable dinner tucked inside the smoky lantern-lit alleyways of postwar Shinjuku, and closes with a free world-class projection mapping light show atop the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building. The pace is relaxed — each stop has ample time to breathe — and the evening finale rewards patience with an atmosphere that few visitors get to witness.

## Itinerary

### Morning / Afternoon — Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (09:30)

[![Shinjuku Gyoen cherry blossoms and lawns](https://cdn.cheapoguides.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2019/10/shinjuku-gypen-cherry-blossoms-iStock-Tsz-Sang-Wong-1024x600.jpg)](https://tokyocheapo.com/place/shinjuku-gyoen-national-garden/ "Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden — Tokyo Cheapo")

**📍 11 Naito-machi, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo 160-0014**

Shinjuku Gyoen is a rare pocket of serenity inside one of the world's busiest cities. At 58.3 hectares, the garden combines three distinct styles — formal French, naturalistic English, and traditional Japanese — meaning there is always something new around the next bend. Enter via the Shinjuku Gate (west side) or Okido Gate (east side near Shinjuku-gyoenmae Station on the Marunouchi Line).

Spend 2–3 hours exploring at a relaxed pace: walk the long French parterre lawns, rest by the Japanese pond, duck into the Victorian-era greenhouse, and find a quiet bench under the trees. Bring a picnic or pick up onigiri and drinks from the convenience store near the entrance. In May the garden is lush green — perfect weather for a long, unhurried walk.

**Budget this stop:** ¥500 entry + ¥500–¥800 if buying snacks or drinks inside

> ⚠️ **Closed Mondays** (or the following Tuesday if Monday is a public holiday). Hours are generally 09:00–18:30 in May. Check the [official NTA website](https://www.env.go.jp/garden/shinjukugyoen/english/) for seasonal updates.

### Dinner — Shinjuku Omoide Yokocho (17:30)

[![Yakitori stalls lit up in Omoide Yokocho at night](https://cdn.cheapoguides.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/05/Omoide-Yokocho-Autumn-iStock-897893036-aon168-1024x600.jpg)](https://tokyocheapo.com/place/omoide-yokocho-piss-alley/ "Omoide Yokocho — Tokyo Cheapo")

**📍 1-2 Nishi-Shinjuku, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo 160-0023**

Tucked just west of Shinjuku Station's JR exit, Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane") is one of Tokyo's most atmospheric eating spots. Around 80 tiny stalls crowd two narrow alleys, most specialising in yakitori — skewered chicken grilled over charcoal — alongside cold Sapporo and Asahi on tap. The alley dates to the postwar black market era and has changed remarkably little since.

Arrive around 17:30 when the stalls are just lighting up their lanterns and the crowds are still manageable. Most stalls seat 6–10 people shoulder-to-shoulder at a wooden counter. Point at what you want, or simply say *yakitori* and let the chef decide. Allow 1.5–2 hours here to eat, drink, and soak in the atmosphere before moving on to the light show.

**Budget this stop:** ¥1,500–¥3,500 per person (food + 2–3 drinks). Most stalls are **cash only** — bring yen.

> 💡 Stalls open around 17:00–18:00 and most run until midnight. Arriving before 19:00 gives you the best chance of a counter seat without a long wait.

### Evening — Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (19:30)

[![Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building exterior at dusk](https://cdn.cheapoguides.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/09/PXL_20230831_071143066.MP_-1024x600.jpg)](https://tokyocheapo.com/place/tokyo-metropolitan-government-building/ "Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building — Tokyo Cheapo")

**📍 2-8-1 Nishi-Shinjuku, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo 163-8001**

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (東京都庁, *Tochō*) is a 243-metre twin-tower landmark designed by Kenzo Tange. Both the North and South observation decks on the 45th floor are **completely free** — one of the best value viewpoints in Tokyo. The South Observatory is open until 22:00 (last entry 21:30), giving you a comfortable window to arrive after dinner.

From roughly sunset onward (around 19:00 in May), the base of the towers is transformed by a nightly **projection mapping light show** that runs every 30 minutes. The show plays across the building's grand stone façade with high-resolution animated visuals — changing themes throughout the year. Watch the show from the plaza below first, then take the elevator up to the observatory for sweeping night views over Shinjuku's neon skyline.

**Budget this stop:** Free.

> 💡 The projection mapping show is best viewed from the open plaza in front of the main tower, not from inside the building. After watching one or two cycles, head up to the observatory for the night view.

## Tips

- **Shinjuku Gyoen is closed on Mondays** (or Tuesday if Monday is a public holiday) — plan accordingly.
- **Omoide Yokocho is cash only** at most stalls. Bring at least ¥3,000–¥4,000 per person in coins and notes before you enter.
- The **projection mapping light show** at the TMG Building runs every ~30 minutes from sunset. In May, sunset is around 18:30–19:00, so the first shows begin around that time.
- The **South Observatory** closes at 22:00 (last entry 21:30) and is free. The North Observatory hours vary — check the [official site](https://www.seisakukikaku.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/about-tokyo/facilities/observatory/) before going.
- The whole day is walkable within Shinjuku once you arrive — but use the metro (Marunouchi Line, Shinjuku-gyoenmae Station) to get from Gyoen to the west-exit area where Omoide Yokocho and the TMG Building are located.
